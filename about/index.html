Our story

Meet Gripp, The Hip Hop Jib Op.

Gerard “Gripp” Richardson is a camera crane operator, a music and media producer, and Managing Director of Groove Logic Group Inc. This is how a one-way ticket turned into fifteen years on the jib.

The one-way ticket

December 15, 2011.

Before the jib, there was music. Gripp Tight Productions LLC has been making music and media since August 2005, and that music background is one of the roots of how Gripp runs a jib today.

On December 15, 2011, he took a one-way flight from Greenville-Spartanburg to Los Angeles to start a film career. No return date. He still celebrates that day as his career birthday as The Hip Hop Jib Op.

The first week

One party. Seven days. A career.

Weeks after landing, a friend brought him to a New Year’s party in Sherman Oaks and introduced him to the person who would open almost every door that followed. What happened next is the shortest version of his whole career:

  1. SaturdayThe New Year’s party. The right introduction.
  2. MondayIn class.
  3. WednesdayThe phone rings.
  4. ThursdayFirst day of work on set.
  5. SaturdayFirst paid job.
Relationships are the channel

Skills built the ship. Relationships cut the channel.

Fifteen years later, more than 90% of Gripp’s work has come through relationships, and many of them are still solid today. When he traced his jobs back one by one, nearly every one led to people he met through that single party, one introduction at a time.

That is the half of the business JODT teaches right alongside the rig: how to become the operator people call back.

The work

From the Olympics to the pyramids.

Gripp went to the 2014 Winter Olympics as an assistant. The people he worked alongside there called him back directly for 2018, 2022 and 2026: four Winter Games in a row.

He has run the jib for livestreams at Egypt’s pyramids with the Red Hot Chili Peppers (2019) and Maroon 5 (2022), every year at Cali Roots in Monterey, and on stages from the Hollywood Bowl and Red Rocks to Wrigley Field and Madison Square Garden, with more in San Francisco and Nashville. One of his reels peaked at 19 million views on Instagram.

Between the big shows are the jobs that keep a career steady: film and TV sets, broadcast work and church productions.

How he teaches

The G.R.I.P.P. Framework.

Running a jib well is more than knowing which knob to turn. It is knowing whether a move belongs in the shot at all. Gripp wrote down how he makes those calls as the G.R.I.P.P. Framework, drawn from music production, video editing, camera crane operation, live production and teaching.

  • GrooveContinuity over time.
  • RhythmCharacter from timing, spacing and energy.
  • InstrumentThe tool your intention moves through.
  • PatternWhat experience teaches you to recognize.
  • PhraseThe meaning your decisions add up to.

G.R.I.P.P. is a practice-based way of thinking about judgment, not a scientific method, and it never overrides equipment specs or safety procedure.

Why JODT

Teaching both halves of the career.

Jib Or Die Tryin’ puts fifteen years of that work in one place: how to build, balance and run the rig safely and smoothly, and how to become the operator people call back. It lives on your phone, with the Master Book, Ask Gripp, and a network of operators that shares real paid work.

He also speaks to young people, creatives, church media teams and business owners about the same idea that built his career: the work you do matters, and so do the people you do it with.
